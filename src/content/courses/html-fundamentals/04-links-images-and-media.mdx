Hyperlinks (`<a>`) and images (`<img>`) turn isolated documents into a connected, visual web. Getting their attributes right directly improves **accessibility** and **Core Web Vitals** (like Cumulative Layout Shift).

## Writing Better Links

Screen-reader users often scan a page by listing all links out of context. That's why link text should make sense on its own.

## Performant, Accessible Images

Every `<img>` should include:
- **`alt`**: Describes the image's purpose. Use an empty `alt=""` for purely decorative images so screen readers skip them.
- **`width` & `height`**: Lets the browser reserve exact space before the image loads, preventing jarring page jumps (CLS).
- **`loading="lazy"`**: Defers off-screen images until the user scrolls near them. (Skip lazy loading on your main above-the-fold hero image!)

<Callout type="tip" title="Use <figure> for Captions">
  Wrap an image and its caption inside <code>&lt;figure&gt;</code> and <code>&lt;figcaption&gt;</code> so assistive technologies associate them together.
</Callout>
